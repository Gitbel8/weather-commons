Realtime Weather · Sketch 13

Aerolectics

What changes when we understand them not as statistics or archival collateral damage, but as movements of air that resist discipline, or, in metaphorical terms, weather modification?

This project looks at how weather and weather phenomena were used by the nuns and priests involved in the trafficking of African girls* and young women*, both as an explanation for their deaths and as a means of discipline. Its starting point is a map that understands the girls* and young women* as weather phenomena, more precisely, movements of air; hence the title, Aerolectics. The term builds on Kamau Brathwaite's concept of tidalectics, his counter-model to linear dialectics, and his attempt to tell the histories of the Caribbean through the movement of the tides. In this sense, Aerolectics seeks to narrate the stories of the African girls* and young women* by observing movements of air, and to learn how to tell nonlinear stories that move like air.

A speculative map. The movements of air are metaphors. Each one begins when she arrives at a convent and lasts until her death. Where no death is recorded, it has not ended.

Sources and limits

Source: Ute Küppers-Braun, Gekauft im Namen des Herrn. Afrikanische Kindersklaven in europäischen Klöstern im 19. Jahrhundert, Münster: Aschendorff 2021. Convents from Appendix 1, all other data from Appendix 2 (pp. 337–367).

The asterisk marks what the archive cannot hold: uncertain names, uncertain spellings, and categories imposed on them.

  • Names. Shown is the name she had before baptism, where the source gives one, with every recorded spelling. X* where only a baptismal name or “NN” is recorded. Racist labels used in the sources are not reproduced. Some names, such as Bakhita (“the lucky one”) or Mabruka (“the blessed one”), may themselves have been given during enslavement.
  • Dates. As recorded, many approximate (“before”, “ca.”, missing days). Durations run from arrival to death and are approximate too.
  • Breath. A cause of death is recorded for only of entries. Of these, name the lungs or breath, and more use terms that most likely mean the same (Schwindsucht, Auszehrung, Blutsturz, Brustleiden). Causes are quoted in the German of the source.
  • Places. Spirals are placed at the town, arranged around it, not at the exact building. Where a town had several convents and the record does not say which, all are named. Noiron, Saint-Étienne, Le Trévoux and Ancizan are located approximately. The outline of the Alps is drawn by hand. Coastline: Natural Earth.
  • Insubric Line. Drawn schematically, after its course from Ivrea via Locarno, the Valtellina and the Tonale Pass. Its continuation, the Periadriatic Seam, runs via Meran, Bruneck (Pustertal line), the Gail valley and the Karawanks. It separates the Southern Alps, on the Adriatic plate, from the rest of the Alps.
  • Gaps. The numbering in the source has gaps, and girls whom missionaries left in Cairo are not part of it. This map shows the 557 recorded entries, not everyone.
